[p1] Intro-Politics:
[p2] The Japanese world, or the world of Yamatai was at its early shape of mid- Classical age by AD220-280, they were in the late “Yayoi era” and Early “Ancient tomb era”. By this, it means the society has three key features. Firstly the existence of “Tribe politics” in their monarchy, the strong and obvious blood links amongst citizens in each Wo city-state, divided them into “Shi”(Clans), each represented by a primary Elder or Parent; Secondly, the presences of mythical influence, even obvious Theocratic Monarchy can be found in the records and archeology results, this  will be explained further below in particle; Thirdly, the lack of sufficient farming methods and political system, limited the scale and manpower in farming and colonization of wild fields, then limited the size of the cities, the nation and eventually their population.
[p3] 
[p4] How should we set Tech levels and inventions for them?
[p5] In the shore of South Japan, by AD220-280, Yamatai and all of her subjects——historians called them Queen’s Kingdom together, were beginners in Iron manufactures. Approximately, Japanese had Iron tools imported from Korea and China since BC500s, by the start_date of the Three Kingdoms period, AD220s, the civilization has learnt how to mine, cast and forge iron tools. This largely improve their agriculture by scale, which is critical. Since there were no evidence Japanese understood how to build complex farmland system, (e.g. watermill and paired irrigation system) no more than simple protective ridges around rice fields and “Slash and burn” farming method.
[p6] So, the lack of skills and technologies to establish sufficient farmlands pushed Ancient Japanese further into ocean, which was the only way to find enough food. The vast amount of fish stock around Japan also attracting them to do so. Therefore, 3 out of 7 city-states mentioned by Chinese records in “the Kingdom of Yamatai” had dependency on fishing, while they “can’t gain enough grain through farming”. Noticeably, Japanese were rice farmers all along, while Northern Chinese in similar latitude were millet and wheat farmers at the time.
[p7] Benefited by fishmen traditions, Japanese were part of those finest sailors and ship builders in East-asia at the time. While the Mid- and Southern Chinese invented Louchuan and Mengchong, two types of large flat bottomed galley ship, the Japanese had a special type of Galley or Boat, which more of less similar to Viking Long Boat without a sail or Polynesian Catamaran (a modern copy displayed in Osaka History Museum), which capable of traveling from Japan directly to Korean south coast. Each of these boats had capacity varies from 10-30 people on board, helped Japanese coast cities at the time trading frequently with ancient Korean kingdoms. Later in the history, by AD397, the skill of making this type of boats and Chinese-design larger war vessels allowed united Japan under Yamato to send a vast invasion army across the Sea of Japan’s Tsushima Strait to Korea, successfully invaded and occupied three southern Kingdoms in the peninsula, and waged a war lasted for decades between the northern Kingdoms(
[p8] Goguryeo by the record) and southern Korean residents. 
[p9] How should we set Cultures for them?
[p10] By the results of modern researches and ancient records, the Yayoi and Tomb era Japan can be divided into mainly two distinguished culture_groups(game concept), which has shown on the descriptions on the map. 
[p11] For the major one, Japanese Culture_group or Wo/Yamatai culture_group, it was a combination of three menthioned cultures in ancient Japanese mythical “History records” and the only recorded Japanese Kingdom of “Wo” or “Yamatai”. The three cultures were Kyushu Culture, Yamato culture and Izumo Culture. Within the three, the existence of Kyushu culture and Yamato culture has been confirmed through archeology, although some may argue they were one. But, the third culture of Izumo, was kinda foggy in history. There were certainly advanced city-states with superior technologies compare to Yamato(not Kyushu or Wo), until they were fallen because of wars between themselves, but it is unclear who they were and how they lived their life. Therefore, according to the study of some modern essays translated about them, Chinese records and Ancient Japanese myths, I named them respectively, along with the Shikoku states, who were flourished parallel with all these cultures, but also lack of marks in the history.
[p12] To the other two of the group, Yamato was the winners’ one, but Kyushu, the Wo was the leading figure of the three. It was not only because they were able to send diplomats to Korea and China, and the archeologists found more complex farmlands and a significant amount of iron and copper ritual tools than the rest of tombs and ruins at the time, suggested a superior civilizations with no peers exited around the area. On the other hand, the Yamato might have been an “abled” civilized culture, with vast potential by its good location about Shinai region of Japan, considerably today’s Nara, Osaka and Wakayama.
